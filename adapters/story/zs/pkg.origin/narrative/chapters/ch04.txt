The door did not open.

Kael knocked again, harder, and then once more with the flat of his fist. The sound traveled down the empty street and died in the shadow of a closed pawnshop. The invitation that had appeared on his Panel the night before had given an address: an old two-story building beside the pawnshop, number twelve, August City. It was supposed to be the future home of Guild Alpha.

It was also a room full of dusty air and nothing else.

He tried the handle. It turned. The door swung inward. Kael stepped into a space lit by a single pale bulb. There were no chairs, no tables, no guild banner. A single envelope lay on the floor, addressed to "First Member." He opened it. Inside was a charter, with a line for a name, a line for a class, and a line for a signature. The signature block was blank.

He folded it into his jacket and left.

At the door, he paused. Somebody knew about Slot Zero. Somebody had left that note in the back of his Panel. And they had sent him here, to an empty room. He touched the back of his Panel through his jacket and felt the folded paper still there. No signature. Just a warning: VERSION is the lock, not the key.

"Tomorrow," he said to no one. "I'll find them."

But tomorrow started earlier than expected.

Mira was already at the training yard behind the old waterworks when Kael arrived, her copper hair in a tight braid and a practice sword balanced across her knees. A fresh scrape ran along her left hand.

"Fell out of bed," she said.

"You were training without me."

"Obviously. I was a hundred XP from level two. I wanted to make up ground." She tilted her head. "You look worse than I feel."

Kael sat on the cracked concrete bench and pulled up his Panel. Blue light spilled across his fingers. He saw the familiar rows: LEVEL 2, XP 305. His stats under that: STR 7, AGI 5, INT 5, VIT 7, WIS 5. Six unspent points sat at the bottom of the screen, waiting. And below them, separate and strange, the extra slot.

VERSION.

"No sleep," he said. "I knocked on the guild's door. No one answered."

"Were you expecting a full army?"

"I was expecting a lock. Not an open door and an empty room."

He closed the Panel and pulled a practice knife from his belt. "Come on. I need XP before the Old Theater splits me in half."

They started with drills. The Panel gave every holder three basic skills early on: Steady, Read, and Recover. The real skill trees did not unlock until level three. Until then, training was mostly raw numbers—learning what your stats meant when translated into sweat and bruises.

Mira triggered Read. A pale tag appeared over her head: MIRA — HUMAN — LV 1. Kael returned the gesture, and his own tag appeared over him: KAEL — HUMAN — LV 2.

"You're a wall," Mira said.

"That's the VIT."

"A slow wall."

"That's the AGI." He grinned. "Come hit me and find out."

They circled each other. Mira moved first, stepping in with a quick diagonal cut. Kael caught it on the flat of his practice knife and shoved her back. She recovered fast and came again, low this time, aiming at his ribs. He dropped his weight and absorbed the blow. The practice edge stung through his jacket.

"Good," he said. "Again."

They traded strikes until their arms burned. Kael's high VIT let him take hits that would have folded another level-one. His STR made every block heavy, every counter strike rough. But Mira was faster, and she used it. She clipped his shoulder, then his hip, then his ear.

His Panel fizzed with small notifications: +2 XP, +4 XP, +3 XP. He checked his total. 357. "This is slow."

"Because you fight like a wall," Mira said. "Try using your eyes, not your ribs."

They reset. Kael remembered the sewer rift, the way the creatures had shifted their weight before they attacked. He watched Mira's shoulders instead of her blade. When she stepped in, he saw the tell, twisted aside, and caught her wrist with the flat of his knife. She stumbled, hit the gravel, and laughed.

His Panel flashed: +12 XP — clean form bonus.

Kael's total hit 369. He pulled Mira up.

"That was ugly," she said.

"That was math."

They drank water. Then they went again. More sparring, more drills, more readings of each other's tags. By the time the sun cleared the fence and turned the gravel bright, Kael's XP had climbed to 425. He was close to level three, but not there yet. Seventy-five points away.

Mira, meanwhile, had stopped checking her notifications. When she finally did, a soft chime filled the yard. Her whole body froze. A second chime. Then a column of light rose from her Panel.

"Oh," she said. "Oh, that's—"

"Level two."

"Level two!" She punched the air. "I got it!"

She opened her Panel and spent her new points with the eager speed of someone who had been waiting. Kael checked the tag over her head: MIRA — HUMAN — LV 2.

"Now you're legal," he said. "If you wanted to go into the Old Theater."

"I did." She closed her Panel. "I told you, I'm not staying behind anymore."

He wanted to argue, but the words felt wrong. She had earned it. And the Old Theater was still open, a depth-two rift waiting in the shadow of its broken marquee. He had been planning to go alone, but alone had gotten him a step closer to death in the sewer mouth.

"Tomorrow," he said. "We'll plan it tonight."

"Deal."

They sat down on the bench for a break. The morning had turned warm. Kael pulled off his jacket and unclenched his fists. Then he reached into his inner pocket and drew out the Rift Core.

It was the size of a plum, black as tar, with a hairline crack of dull red running through its center. It was the rare drop from the rift under the sewer mouth, the thing that had kept appearing in his inventory even when he told it to stay. It was also the reason his Panel had felt wrong ever since.

Mira looked at it. "You carry that around?"

"Where else would I put it? There's no vault for rift stuff."

"Okay, but—" She reached out. The core was cold in her hand. She turned it over. "It feels heavy. Like there's something inside."

"Watch."

Kael took the core back and opened his Panel. The translucent blue screen floated just above his palm. He held the core near the top of the screen. Nothing. He moved it lower, toward his character sheet. Still nothing.

Then he moved it to the bottom edge, where the extra slot sat alone.

A flush of warmth ran up his wrist. Not the warmth of a sun-heated stone. It was a sharp, focused heat, as if the core had recognized something. It pulsed once. The red crack inside it glowed brighter for half a second.

And in Slot Zero, the word VERSION flickered from black to white, then back.

Mira leaned in. "Did it just—"

"Yeah." Kael kept the core steady over the slot. The heat held, patient, pulsing. "It's reacting to the slot. Not to the rest of the Panel."

"The extra slot."

"Slot Zero."

Mira moved the core herself. It stayed cold in her fingers. She held it near her own Panel, over her skill list, over her character sheet. Nothing. She handed it back to Kael. "It's not reacting to the System. It's reacting to you. Or to that word."

"VERSION." Kael closed his hand around the core. The warmth faded slowly, like water draining from a cup. "The note said that word was the lock. Not the key."

"So what's the key?"

"I don't know. But the core knows something. It went hot the second it touched Slot Zero. Maybe the core is a key. Maybe the slot is a door."

He put the core back in his pocket. The afterimage of the word VERSION still hung in his vision.

"Who left the note?" Mira asked.

"I don't know. No signature. No clue. Just the guild name and the address."

"And an empty room with a charter."

"Exactly."

"Someone wants you to build the guild."

Kael looked at her. The thought settled into him, heavy and strange. "Or they wanted me to find the room and realize there's no one to trust."

"That's dark."

"The System is dark." He stood up, flexing his sore shoulder. "The version is 0.9.1. But Slot Zero only says VERSION. No number. No date. It's a question, not an answer."

Mira stood too. "You have a rift core that warms up when you touch it. You have a panel with an extra slot that shows a word nobody else sees. You have an invitation to a guild that doesn't exist yet. You're not normal, Kael."

"Thanks."

"I mean it as a compliment." She smiled. "Now teach me how to hit as hard as you do."

The rest of the morning passed in a blur of drills and bruises. By noon, Kael's XP was still 425—training gave less after the edge wore off—but Mira's movements had sharpened. Her level-two points made a difference. She was quicker, cleaner, harder to read.

They stopped under the shade of a dying elm. Kael pulled up his Panel one last time. Level 2. XP 425. Seventy-five points to level three. The skill trees would unlock when he leveled. Maybe then he would get something useful—an active skill, a weapon skill, anything.

Maybe then the word VERSION would make sense.

He wanted to believe that. But the Rift Core had warmed in his hand as if it recognized a name. And names, in the System, were never free.

"Old Theater tomorrow," Mira said.

"Old Theater tomorrow." He looked toward the east, where the theater's ruined silhouette hunched behind the rooftops. "Depth two. We'll need better gear, better positioning, and maybe a plan that doesn't end with me in the hospital."

"We'll get you to level three first."

"That's the plan."

He touched his jacket pocket. The Rift Core was still warm. The folded charter was still there. And behind him, in the back of his Panel, the extra slot waited with its single word.

VERSION.

A question, not an answer.

He was going to find the person who had written it. And if the Old Theater got in his way, he would cut through it.
