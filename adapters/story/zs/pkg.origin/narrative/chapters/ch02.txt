Kael let out a breath he had forgotten he was holding. The green light under the manhole cover was gone, but the darkness below still pulsed once, slowly, like something had turned over in its sleep.

“So,” Mira said, “we’re doing this.”

Her Panel had already lit up. It cast a faint blue glow across her face from the corner of her eye, the same way his did. She read aloud: “‘Clear Rift #1. Objective: Enter Rift · Sewer Mouth. Defeat rift beast. Claim Rift Core. Rewards: one hundred twenty XP, First Clear Bonus.’” She looked at him. “That’s not a tutorial. That’s a summons.”

Kael’s own Panel opened in front of him. The quest was waiting.QUEST: CLEAR RIFT #1 (0/1)OBJECTIVE: ENTER RIFT · SEWER MOUTHOBJECTIVE: DEFEAT RIFT BEAST(S)OBJECTIVE: CLAIM RIFT CORE

Below every quest line was the same thin grey footnote: “System Version 0.9.0 (BETA). Not a final experience.”

“Beta,” Kael muttered. “The world’s falling apart and the prompt says beta.”

The manhole cover had been moved aside before they arrived. Holes in the world did not wait for permission. He crouched at the edge, looking down at the ladder. The first rung was crusted with rust, the rest lost in shadows. The smell was not just sewer. It was wet soil, copper, burnt sugar, and something like old meat. The air rose warm from below, and the warmth felt wrong—the night above was cold, sea wind straight off the bay.

“Kael.” Mira’s voice had gone quiet. “My Panel says ‘level recommendation: N/A.’ What does that mean?”

“It means the System doesn’t know what’s down there either.” He gripped the ladder. “Stay behind me. Don’t touch anything that glows.”

“What if the wall glows?”

“Then don’t touch the wall.”

He climbed down.

The ladder took them lower than it should have. August City’s sewers were old brick, narrow, with waterlines stained brown along the walls. Kael had been in them before, when he was twelve, chasing a dog into the drain tunnel. This was not the same place. The bricks were older, darker, and the mortar had a greenish tint. The angle of the floor sloped left, then right, then left again, as though the tunnel itself had been folded.

Mira’s flashlight cut a white cone through the dark. Dust motes swam in it, but they were not dust. They were tiny grey spores, floating upward against gravity.

The air changed. Kael’s ears popped.

“Pressure drop,” Mira said. “We’re not under the street anymore.”

Ahead of them, the brick walls gave way to packed brown earth. Fine roots threaded through the ceiling like veins. A patch of blue-white fungus bloomed in a spiral at the center of the floor. It pulsed. Not with light—with movement, as though the fungus was breathing.

Mira stopped. “There.”

A second patch of fungus on the ceiling. A third, halfway up the wall, overgrown with a grey membrane. The membrane rippled once, then went still.

“It’s a nest,” she said.

“No. It’s the rift.” Kael stepped past the first spiral. The root walls squeezed inward, then opened into a chamber.

The room was a collapsed drainage tank. The ceiling had crumbled in, and the roots had taken over the top, weaving into a dense mat. In the center of the room was a mound of wet leaves, black plastic, and bones. The bones were small—rat bones, bird bones. But the shape of the nest was too large, as if something had been using it for a bed.

Kael raised his hand. “Quiet.”

From the nest came a sound. A long, bubbling hiss that rose and fell, like a respirator in the dark.

Then the Bloatrat lifted its head.

It had been a rat, once. Now it was the size of a boar. Its body was swollen, the skin stretched so tight it was translucent. Loose yellow fluid sloshed inside it as it shifted. Its face was still rat-like, with a long skull and dagger teeth, but the eyes were sunk deep, glowing orange. Its forelegs were short, its hind legs long and muscled. A tail like a wet rope dragged behind it, and the tip flicked with a sound like cracking knuckles.

His Panel wrote the name in red letters: BLOATRAT, LV2.

Mira moved first. She stepped sideways, raising a short claw hammer she had taken from the tool pouch at her belt. “One. Only one.”

“There were two,” Kael said. “I saw eyes in the tunnel behind it.”

The Bloatrat hissed again, and the roots around the nest shivered.

“Don’t let it get between us and the tunnel,” Mira said.

“I know.”

Kael’s weapon was a length of iron pipe he had found on the dock floor—the kind used to replace plumbing, now with a jagged, flat end that could work as a blade if swung hard enough. He held it low, point forward.

The rat lunged.

It was faster than its size should have allowed. A rolling charge, belly low to the ground, legs churning. The nest exploded behind it. Kael planted his feet and thrust the pipe forward. It caught the rat in the shoulder, skidding off the swollen skin with a wet sound. The rat twisted and bit the pipe—teeth sparking against iron—and wrenched it out of his hands.

“Kael!” Mira swung.

Her hammer connected with the rat’s face. A wet, snapping sound. The Bloatrat squealed and reeled, one eye blinking, black fluid dripping from its nose. But it did not fall.

Kael grabbed the pipe from the floor, scrambled backward, and got his feet under him.

The rat shook its head and looked at him with its one good eye.

“Its skin is loose,” Kael said. “It’s containing something. Hit it harder.”

The rat charged again.

This time it went for Mira. She sidestepped, brought the hammer down on its back, and the blow sank into a shallow pocket of fluid under the skin. The creature burst there. A spray of green-black liquid pocked her jacket with hissing holes. Mira gritted her teeth and swung again.

The rat whirled, tail whipping. The tail caught her ankle, ripped her foot out from under her. She went down hard on the packed earth, the flashlight spinning away, leaving them in blue fungal glow.

“Mira!”

“I’m fine! Kill it!”

Her hammer was still in her hand. She rolled to her knees and swung upward as the rat lunged at her torso. The blow caught it under the jaw, snapping its head up.

Kael was already moving.

The Bloatrat turned, its belly rippling, mouth open, teeth aimed at Mira’s face. Kael drove the pipe rod into its throat, angled down, through the loose skin, through the meat beneath, until the jagged point punched out the back of its neck.

It did not die cleanly. The rat thrashed, yelping, clawing the air. Kael leaned into the pipe, twisted it, and shoved until the creature’s body went still.

Then he pulled the pipe out. It slid free with a wet, sucking sound. The rat collapsed, deflating like a pierced balloon, its skin folding over what was left.

Kael stood there, breathing through his mouth, hands shaking.

Mira was already sitting up, checking her shoulder. Claw marks ran down from her collarbone to her elbow, shallow but dark, already blackening at the edges.

“Is that poison?” he asked.

“No.” She touched it and winced. “Not poison. It’s just… irritation. System says nothing. I’ll live.” She looked at the fallen rat. “Who gets the kill credit?”

A chime answered for them.

Kael’s Panel lit up. A blue banner rolled through his vision, quiet and smooth:

KILL CREDIT: BLOATRAT (LV2) — +40 XPQUEST REWARD: CLEAR RIFT #1 — +120 XPFIRST CLEAR BONUS — +25 XP

His XP bar, sitting at 120 from earlier, jumped to 305. The bar shimmered, emptied, and filled again with a brighter gold.LEVEL UP.LEVEL 2.STAT POINTS GRANTED: +5.

Kael blinked. His vision froze for a second as numbers rearranged. When the moment passed, his stats hovered in the corner: STR 5, AGI 5, INT 5, VIT 5, WIS 5, with 10 unspent stat points now waiting in the pool.

“You leveled,” Mira said flatly.

“Yeah.”

“Show-offs get hit.” She stood, brushing dirt from her jacket. “Spend it before you forget.”

Kael did. He opened the stat allocation with a thought, not moving his hands. Two points went into Strength. Two into Vitality. The remaining six stayed unspent—what the Panel called “reserve points,” the ones that could be assigned later.

STR 7. AGI 5. INT 5. VIT 7. WIS 5.

The number in the ledger still balanced. Thirty points at level one, thirty-five at level two. The math was clean. He exhaled.

Mira had opened her own Panel. Her XP bar had moved a little, but she had not leveled. “I did the damage. You got the credit.” She didn’t sound bitter, just tired. “Next one’s mine.”

“Deal.”

They checked the nest.

The Bloatrat’s body had deflated into a thin skin, and the roots beneath it were already grey, decaying. In the center of the nest, half-buried in the dead roots, something pulsed with a cool blue light.

Kael crouched. The object was fist-sized, smooth, black at the core but wrapped in layers of luminous blue lines, like a map of veins drawn in glass. The lines moved. They flowed around the stone in slow loops, and the fungus on the ceiling shrank away from it.

His Panel wrote, in grey letters:

RIFT CORE ACQUIRED.RARITY: RARE.BINDING: NONE.

A second line followed:RIFT #1 CLEARED.STATUS: CLOSED.

Mira stepped closer, inspecting the stone over his shoulder. “Is that the heart of the rift?”

“I don’t know what it is,” Kael said. “But the System wants it. It’s the first rare drop in August City.” He picked it up. It was warm, and the resistance was strange—solid, yet soft, as if his fingers were pushing through jelly.

“Don’t keep it,” Mira said. “We’d be targets.”

“I’m not keeping it. I’m putting it in the guild bank.”

“What guild?”

“Guild Alpha.” He stood, slipping the core into the inner pocket of his jacket. It pulsed once against his ribs. “Or it will be. The Panel says guilds can’t register until level five. So I’m starting it early.”

Mira’s eyebrows went up. “First member?”

“If you want it.”

“I don’t like the name,” she said, after a pause. “But I’d rather be in a bad-named guild than nowhere. Second member.”

“Then it’s official.” Kael almost smiled. “Two members. Zero enemies. One dead rat.”

They turned back the way they had come. The tunnel had changed while they were inside. The roots were pulling back, fading into dust. The brick walls returned, damp and ordinary. The fungal glow died behind them, one spiral at a time. The smell of the rift faded, replaced by the familiar city smell of diesel, rain and rot.

They climbed the ladder in silence. At the top, Kael pushed the manhole cover aside and they crawled out into August City’s cold, clean night.

The streetlight above had come on. A fog was rolling in from the bay, muffling the far-off sound of shipping horns. Mira pulled her jacket tight and looked down at her shoulder. The claw marks had scabbed over already.

“We need to be stronger before the next one,” she said.

“We will be.”

Kael closed his eyes for half a second. His Panel was still open behind his lids: XP 305, LEVEL 2, SLOT ZERO waiting near the top.

It was still blank.

No label. No number. Just an empty space that had no business being there.

As he watched, the cursor in Slot Zero blinked once.

Then again.

Mira was already starting toward the street, her boots ringing on the wet pavement. “Come on. You can brood at home.”

“I’m not brooding.”

“You’re staring at your Panel like it just told you a lie.”

Kael opened his mouth to argue. Then he stopped.

She wasn’t wrong.

The cursor blinked one more time, as if it agreed with her.

Still blank. Still waiting.

Whatever it was, it had noticed him.

And it was patient.
